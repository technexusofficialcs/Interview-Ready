F. RED FLAGS & HIDDEN REJECTION REASONS
========================================

The video highlights several hidden rejection triggers.

1. NO PORTFOLIO / SHOWREEL WHEN THE ROLE REQUIRES DEMONSTRABLE WORK

For creative roles, especially video editing roles, absence of a portfolio or
showreel can be a major problem because the recruiter cannot quickly inspect
actual work.

Workshop takeaway:
If a JD explicitly requires a portfolio/showreel, provide one when you
actually have relevant work to show.

2. TOOL MISMATCH

If the JD explicitly asks for a professional tool and the resume only lists a
different or more basic tool, this can create a mismatch.

Example shown in the video:
- JD: Premiere Pro / professional desktop editing workflow
- Resume: only CapCut

The video argues that this can make a recruiter question whether the candidate
knows the required tool or has the expected production workflow.

IMPORTANT:
Do not falsely claim proficiency in a tool you do not know.
If you genuinely use the requested tool, make that experience visible.

3. TASK-BASED BULLETS WITH NO IMPACT

If all bullets simply describe tasks and do not communicate growth, impact,
results, or outcomes, the resume can look like a list of duties rather than
evidence of contribution.

Example problem:
- "Edited videos."
- "Managed social media."
- "Worked on projects."

Better approach:
Explain what was done, how it was done, and the result — when the result is
known and can be verified.

4. LANGUAGE REQUIREMENTS

If a JD explicitly requires a language, the resume should provide evidence of
that ability when the candidate genuinely has it.

Do not claim language proficiency that you do not have.


G. BULLET-POINT REWRITING — GOOGLE XYZ FORMULA EXAMPLE

"Accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z."

X = What was accomplished

Z = How it was accomplished

- Use strong action verbs.
- Communicate measurable outcomes where real evidence exists.
- Make bullets sound concise, modern, strategic, and recruiter-friendly.
- Prioritize impact, scale, efficiency, leadership, revenue growth, customer
  results, operational improvements, or performance outcomes where relevant.
- Optimize for both recruiters and ATS systems.
- Explain briefly why each rewritten bullet is stronger than the original.

PROMPT
------

Act as a senior recruiter, ATS specialist, and professional resume writer who
hires students and early-career candidates for internships and entry-level
technology roles.

I will provide:
1. My current resume OR my raw career information.
2. The complete job description.

Your task is to take me from diagnosis to a final ATS-friendly resume.

PHASE 1 — UNDERSTAND THE JD
Extract:
- Must-have requirements
- Nice-to-have requirements
- Technical skills
- Tools / software
- Responsibilities
- Domain keywords
- Soft skills
- Experience requirements
- Education requirements

PHASE 2 — AUDIT MY RESUME
Tell me:
- What matches strongly
- What matches partially
- What is missing
- What is irrelevant
- What an ATS may extract
- What a recruiter may notice quickly
- What formatting may interfere with parsing

PHASE 3 — KEYWORD GAP ANALYSIS
Create:
Keyword / Requirement | Importance | Present? | Evidence | Gap | Action

Use only keywords that are genuinely relevant to the JD.
Do not add skills that I do not possess.

PHASE 4 — CONTENT REWRITE
Rewrite weak bullets using:
ACTION + TECHNICAL WORK + CONTEXT + REAL RESULT/IMPACT

Use XYZ when a real metric exists:
ACCOMPLISHED X + MEASURED BY Y + BY DOING Z

Never invent metrics.
If a real number would help, ask me for it.

PHASE 5 — PROJECT OPTIMIZATION
Make projects demonstrate technical ability through:
- What I built
- Technologies used
- Important implementation details
- My individual contribution
- Genuine results

PHASE 6 — FINAL RESUME
Create a clean, professional, single-column resume with standard headings.
Avoid tables, graphics, icons, text boxes, and unnecessary design elements.
Keep it to one page when realistically possible for an internship candidate.

- ATS parsing

The goal is not to make the resume sound impressive.

Instead of saying:

2. AI can improve communication.

The strongest workflow is:

YOUR EXPERIENCE

      ↓

      ↓
